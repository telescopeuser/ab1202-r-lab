How to use this lab

RStudio is the main way to do the lab (the R handout in your Week Pack and the R Recipes file). This page is the alternative when RStudio is not available: it runs R in your browser (webR), with nothing to install. The code is the same, starting with the same first line, hotels <- read.csv("hotels.csv").

  • Press Run Code on each cell. Change the code and run it again; you cannot break anything.
  • Step 1 is always the same line: hotels <- read.csv("hotels.csv"). On this page it runs by itself when the page opens; wait for the first cell to show the size of the data.
  • The tasks are the same as in your R handout, with the same numbers (R 7.1, R 7.2, …) and the same words. Your tier is R 7.6: Core, Applied or Challenge, same data, same idea, different depth.
  • Stuck on a tier task? Open its Hint; then try again before you open its Answer.

Core. The 95% interval for the average rate from the random sample of 30 (R 7.2), by hand and with t.test(). Then draw a sample of 120 with set.seed(1) and compare the margins.

Your steps:

  1. set.seed(1), then a sample of 30 room rates.
  2. The standard error, the t value with qt() and \(n - 1\) degrees of freedom, then the two ends of the interval.
  3. The same interval in one line with t.test().
  4. set.seed(1) again; a sample of 120; its interval. Compare the two widths.
TipHint

set.seed(1); x120 <- sample(hotels$adr, 120), then t.test(x120)$conf.int. The margin is half the width.

Applied. The 95% interval for the share that cancel, from a random sample of 200 (R 7.5). Does it contain the share in the whole file? Then: how many bookings for ±3 points, using your \(\hat p\)?

Your steps:

  1. set.seed(1), then a sample of 200 values of is_canceled, and its share.
  2. The standard error of a share, then the share plus and minus 1.96 standard errors.
  3. Compare the interval with the share in the whole file (the truth, known here).
  4. The sample size for ±3 points, with ceiling().
TipHint

ceiling(1.96^2 * phat * (1 - phat) / 0.03^2).

Challenge. How often does a 95% interval catch \(\mu\)? Run the code below (samples of 10). Compare the \(z\) shortcut (1.96 with \(s\)) with \(t\). Then change 10 to 30 in all three places, and then to 100. How does the gap between the two methods change, and why? (Think about what \(t^*\) does as \(n\) grows.)

TipHint

mean(z_hits) and mean(t_hits) give the share of the 10,000 intervals that caught the truth, for each method.

Stretch (any tier, if you finish early). The bootstrap: resample the sample of 30 from R 7.1 (x) with replacement, 10,000 times: start with set.seed(7) (so your numbers match the answer), then boot <- numeric(10000), then a for loop with boot[i] <- mean(sample(x, replace = TRUE)), then quantile(boot, c(0.025, 0.975)). Compare it with the \(t\) interval.

Check your AI assistant (5 min)

AI is welcome as a study partner. Ask it, then check it in R.

NoteType this to your AI assistant

“I have a 95% confidence interval for the mean room rate of 90.80 to 121.77 euros. Does that mean 95% of guests paid between 90.80 and 121.77 euros?”

“How many people must a poll include to estimate a share to within 3 percentage points, 95% of the time?”

Check the first answer: the interval is about the average rate, not about individual guests (in the file, only 26% of bookings have a rate in that range). Check the second: ceiling(1.96^2 * 0.25 / 0.03^2) gives 1,068. AI says, R checks.

Commands to know

These can be assessed in the quiz: qt(0.975, df = n - 1), qnorm(0.975), t.test(x)$conf.int (and conf.level =), sd(x) / sqrt(n), the proportion interval (se_p <- sqrt(phat * (1 - phat) / n), then phat - 1.96 * se_p and phat + 1.96 * se_p), and ceiling() for a sample size. Remember: 95% describes the method; the interval is about the average, not single values.
