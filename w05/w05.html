How to use this lab

RStudio is the main way to do the lab (the R handout in your Week Pack and the R Recipes file). This page is the alternative when RStudio is not available: it runs R in your browser (webR), with nothing to install. The code is the same, starting with the same first line, hotels <- read.csv("hotels.csv").

  • Press Run Code on each cell. Change the code and run it again; you cannot break anything.
  • Step 1 is always the same line: hotels <- read.csv("hotels.csv"). On this page it runs by itself when the page opens; wait for the first cell to show the size of the data.
  • The tasks are the same as in your R handout, with the same numbers (R 5.1, R 5.2, …) and the same words. Your tier is R 5.6: Core, Applied or Challenge, same data, same idea, different depth.
  • Stuck on a tier task? Open its Hint, then try again. The answers are at the end of your R handout (Part 2 of the Week Pack).

Core. Find \(r\) for lead time and cancelling, and the cancellation share in each lead-time band (R 5.1–5.2). Then find \(r\) for the resort only.

Your steps:

  1. The correlation of lead time and cancelling for all bookings: cor() of the two columns.

TipHint

Applied. From the joint table (R 5.3), find \(P(\text{cancelled})\), \(P(\text{cancelled} \mid 2 \text{ or more requests})\) and the covariance by hand: \(E(XY) - E(X)E(Y)\).

Your steps:

  1. Build the joint table as in R 5.3: the lists p_stayed and p_cancelled, one value for 0, 1 and 2 or more requests.
  2. \(P(\text{cancelled})\): add up p_cancelled.
  3. \(P(\text{cancelled} \mid 2 \text{ or more})\): the “2 or more” cell of p_cancelled over its row total.
  4. \(E(XY) = 1 \times P(1, 1) + 2 \times P(2, 1)\), then the covariance by hand: \(E(XY) - E(X)E(Y)\).
  5. Check it with cov().
TipHint

\(E(XY) = 1 \times P(1, 1) + 2 \times P(2, 1)\), since every other cell has \(X = 0\) or \(Y = 0\).

NoteType this to your AI assistant

“Two variables have a correlation of 0. Does that mean they are unrelated? Give an example.”

“Two hotels’ monthly revenues have SDs of 240 and 290 thousand euros and a correlation of 0.5. What is the SD of their total?”

Check the first answer: a correlation of 0 rules out only a straight-line link (panel D in class). Check the second: \(\sqrt{240^2 + 290^2 + 2 \times 0.5 \times 240 \times 290} = 459.7\); if it says \(240 + 290 = 530\), it has added the SDs. AI says, R checks.
