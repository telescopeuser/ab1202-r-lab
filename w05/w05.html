R 5.3 · A joint distribution and its covariance

With your lecturer

NoteYou should see

table() counts each pair of values; dividing by all bookings gives the joint probabilities (they add to 1). A row or column total is a marginal; a cell divided by its row total is a conditional. The covariance and the correlation are negative: more requests go with fewer cancellations.

Check your AI assistant (5 min)

AI is welcome as a study partner. Ask it, then check it in R.

NoteType this to your AI assistant

“Two variables have a correlation of 0. Does that mean they are unrelated? Give an example.”

“Two hotels’ monthly revenues have SDs of 240 and 290 thousand euros and a correlation of 0.5. What is the SD of their total? Give 2 decimal places.”

Check the first answer: a correlation of 0 rules out only a straight-line link (a curved link can have \(r = 0\)). Check the second: \(\sqrt{240^2 + 290^2 + 2 \times 0.5 \times 240 \times 290} = 459.67\); if it says \(240 + 290 = 530\), it has added the SDs. AI says, R checks.

Commands to know

In scope (you run them in this week’s tasks and sets): cov(), cor(), plot(x, y), hist(), a joint table of counts with table() (divide by nrow(hotels) for the joint probabilities), and the share in a band, such as mean(hotels$is_canceled[hotels$lead_time <= 30]). Remember: \(r = 0\) means no straight-line link; a correlation is not a cause.
