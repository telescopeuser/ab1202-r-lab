How to use this lab

RStudio is the main way to do the lab (the R handout in your Week Pack and the R Recipes file). This page is the alternative when RStudio is not available: it runs R in your browser (webR), with nothing to install. The code is the same, starting with the same first line, hotels <- read.csv("hotels.csv").

  • Press Run Code on each cell. Change the code and run it again; you cannot break anything.
  • Step 1 is always the same line: hotels <- read.csv("hotels.csv"). On this page it runs by itself when the page opens; wait for the first cell to show the size of the data.
  • The tasks are the same as in your R handout, with the same numbers (R 2.1, R 2.2, …) and the same words. Your tier is R 2.6: Core, Applied or Challenge, same data, same idea, different depth.
  • Stuck on a tier task? Open its Hint; then try again before you open its Answer.

R 2.2 · Conditional shares: condition on the row (block 3, with your lecturer)

NoteYou should see

In each row, p_cancelled and p_stayed add to 1. Read the p_cancelled column: \(P(\text{cancel} \mid \text{deposit type})\). Non-refundable bookings cancel far more (99.4%): that is last week’s closing question answered.

R 2.3 · The other way round: condition on the column (block 3, with your lecturer)

NoteYou should see

Now each column adds to 1. Read the given_cancelled column: of all cancellations, only 32.9% were non-refundable. \(P(A \mid B)\) and \(P(B \mid A)\) are different numbers.

R 2.4 · Independent or not? (6 min)

NoteChange one thing

Change "City Hotel" to "Resort Hotel" in the city <- line, run that line, then run the last two lines again. (The name city now holds the resort bookings.) Is P(Resort and cancelled) equal to P(Resort) × P(cancelled)?

NoteYou should see

If hotel and outcome were independent, \(P(\text{cancel} \mid \text{City})\) would equal \(P(\text{cancel})\), and the last two numbers would be equal. They are not.

R 2.5 · Total probability and Bayes’ rule (6 min)

NoteChange one thing

In the Bayes card, change "Non Refund" to "Refundable" in all three places. Do Bayes’ rule and counting agree on P(refundable | cancelled)?

NoteYou should see

The first number is \(P(\text{cancel})\) rebuilt from the deposit groups. The last two are the same: Bayes’ rule and counting in the table agree.

R 2.6 · Your tier (in pairs)

Pick one. Same data, same idea, different depth. Write your answers in your own notes; nothing here is submitted. Try first, then check: the answers are at the end of your R handout (Week Pack).

Core. Find \(P(\text{cancel} \mid \text{Resort})\). Then compare first-time guests with returning guests: who cancels more?

TipHint

mean(hotels$is_canceled[hotels$is_repeated_guest == 0]) for first stays; change 0 to 1 for returning guests.

Applied. Are hotel and cancellation independent? Check both ways (R 2.4). Then: do bookings with at least one special request cancel less?

TipHint

has_request <- hotels$total_of_special_requests > 0, then mean(hotels$is_canceled[has_request]); and !has_request for bookings without one.

Challenge. Find \(P(\text{non-refundable} \mid \text{cancel})\) by Bayes’ rule and by counting (R 2.5). Then find out who the non-refundable bookings are: which hotel, which market segment, how far ahead.

TipHint

nr <- hotels[hotels$deposit_type == "Non Refund", ], then mean(nr$hotel == "City Hotel"), and a for loop over the market segments as in the code below.

Stretch (any tier, if you finish early). Play the three doors with four doors: the host opens one empty door; you switch to one of the two other closed doors at random. What is your chance of winning by switching? Check it with a simulation.

Check your AI assistant (5 min)

AI is welcome as a study partner. Ask it, then check it in R.

NoteType this to your AI assistant

“In a hotel’s data, 99% of bookings with a non-refundable deposit were cancelled. What share of all cancellations had a non-refundable deposit?”

“Explain the difference between independent and mutually exclusive events, with a hotel example, in three sentences.”

The first question cannot be answered without more information (it needs \(P(\text{non-refundable})\) and \(P(\text{cancel})\)). If the assistant gives 99%, it has swapped the conditional. AI says, R checks.

Commands to know

These can be assessed in the quiz: sum() on a test to count (sum(rows & hotels$is_canceled == 1)); a count divided by its row total or its column total for a conditional probability; mean(hotels$is_canceled[hotels$hotel == "City Hotel"]) for one group’s share; mean(a & b) for a joint share. Also useful: sample() with set.seed() for simulation.
