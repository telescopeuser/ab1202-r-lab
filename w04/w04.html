R 4.5 · Totals and linear changes

On your own · about 6 minutes

NoteChange one thing

In the card for the total, change 20 to 80 in all three lines. How many times larger is the SD of the total than for 20 bookings? (A whole number.)

NoteYou should see

For independent bookings the variances add, so the SD of the total is the square root of 20 times one booking’s SD: the second number, not the third. A fee added to every booking moves the mean, not the SD.

R 4.7 · Simulate to check

With the class

The simulation pattern. The next card has the course’s first loop. You will use the same four parts in every simulation to Week 12:

  1. runs <- 10000: how many times to repeat.
  2. results <- numeric(runs): an empty vector of numbers, one place per run.
  3. for (i in 1:runs) { ... }: R runs the lines inside the braces once for each i from 1 to runs.
  4. results[i] <- ...: each run’s result goes in its own place, i. After the loop, mean(results) and sd(results) summarise all the runs.
NoteChange one thing

In the tonight line, change 20 to 80, run the whole card again from set.seed(6), and compare the simulated SD with sqrt(80) * sd(real), both in euros to 2 decimal places.

NoteYou should see

Ordering 90 earns more on average than ordering the average demand, 70: running out costs 4 euros of profit, a leftover only 1.

NoteYou should see

For real nights, the simulated mean and SD match 20 times the mean and the square root of 20 times the SD: the rules for totals hold. The SD here (€1,028.96) is larger than R 4.5’s €714.59 because real bookings vary more than R 4.5’s two-value model: that model gives every guest who comes the same revenue, R, but real bookings differ in nights and room rate.

Commands to know

In scope (you run them in this week’s tasks and sets): sum(x * px) for an expected value, sqrt(sum((x - EX)^2 * px)) for an SD, making a new column with $, mean(), pmin(), and the simulation pattern (runs, results <- numeric(runs), for (i in 1:runs) { ... }, results[i] <- ...) with sample(). Remember: means add; for independent variables, variances add, not SDs.
