How to use this lab

RStudio is the main way to do the lab (the R handout in your Week Pack and the R Recipes file). This page is the alternative when RStudio is not available: it runs R in your browser (webR), with nothing to install. The code is the same, starting with the same first line, hotels <- read.csv("hotels.csv").

  • Press Run Code on each cell. Change the code and run it again; you cannot break anything.
  • Step 1 is always the same line: hotels <- read.csv("hotels.csv"). On this page it runs by itself when the page opens; wait for the first cell to show the size of the data.
  • The tasks are the same as in your R handout, with the same numbers (R 6.1, R 6.2, …) and the same words. Your tier is R 6.5: Core, Applied or Challenge, same data, same idea, different depth.

Core. Simulate the sampling distribution of the average rate for samples of 30 (R 6.2), and compare its SD with \(\sigma/\sqrt{30}\) (2 decimal places). Then do it for samples of 120.

Your steps:

  1. set.seed(6), then the one simulation pattern: 10,000 averages of samples of 30, kept in means30 (as in R 6.2).
  2. sd() of means30 against sigma / sqrt(30).
  3. set.seed(6) again; the same for samples of 120, in means120; compare with sigma / sqrt(120).
TipHint

the second time, put 120 inside sample(), keep the averages in means120, and compare sd(means120) with sigma / sqrt(120).

Applied. Repeat R 6.4 for samples of 40 lead times. Find \(P(\bar X > 120)\) by the CLT and by simulation (3 decimal places).

Your steps:

  1. set.seed(6), then the one simulation pattern: 10,000 averages of samples of 40 lead times, kept in lead40; draw their histogram.
  2. \(P(\bar X > 120)\) from the CLT, with pnorm().
  3. The same share from the simulation.
TipHint

the CLT’s normal curve has mean mean(hotels$lead_time) and SD sd(hotels$lead_time) / sqrt(40); the simulation’s answer is mean(lead40 > 120).

R 6.6 · Bigger is not better if biased

With the class

NoteYou should see

August’s bookings are a huge sample, far outside the range of random samples of only 100. The standard error measures chance error; it cannot measure bias.

Check your AI assistant (5 min)

AI is welcome as a study partner. Ask it, then check it in R.

NoteType this to your AI assistant

“Does the central limit theorem mean that my data become normal when I collect a large sample?”

“Room rates have an SD of 46.71 euros. What is the standard error of the average of 400 bookings? Give 2 decimal places.”

Check the first answer: the CLT is about the distribution of the average, not the data (R 6.4: single lead times stay right-skewed). Check the second: \(46.71/\sqrt{400} = 2.34\), which sigma / sqrt(400) confirms. AI says, R checks.

Commands to know

In scope (you run them in this week’s tasks and sets): set.seed(), sample(x, n), the simulation pattern (runs <- 10000, an empty vector with numeric(runs), and for (i in 1:runs) { filling place i), sigma / sqrt(n) for the standard error, hist(), pnorm() for a probability about an average, quantile() and length(). Remember: the standard error is for an average; a big biased sample is still biased.
